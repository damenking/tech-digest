Pentagon wants $30M for AI-powered lie detector program
The US Department of Defense has submitted a budget request for $30.3 million over five years to develop what it's calling 'Polygraph+' or 'Polygraph Next' — an AI and machine learning-enhanced lie detection system that goes beyond traditional polygraphs. The program would combine AI-based scoring algorithms with 'standoff sensing,' a technique that attempts to detect physiological stress indicators without requiring the subject to be physically wired up, which is a significant capability expansion over conventional polygraphs. The scientific foundation for lie detection technology has always been shaky — traditional polygraphs are inadmissible in most courts precisely because they're unreliable — and layering AI on top of fundamentally contested science doesn't automatically solve the underlying validity problem. There's a real risk that AI-enhanced systems will produce more confident-seeming outputs that are actually no more accurate, while the 'black box' nature of ML models makes it harder to challenge their conclusions. The standoff sensing component is particularly interesting from a civil liberties perspective, as it could theoretically be deployed without subjects' knowledge or consent in environments like border crossings or security checkpoints. For anyone interested in AI safety and ethics in government applications, this is worth tracking — it sits at the intersection of unproven science, significant government spending, and potential civil liberties implications.
