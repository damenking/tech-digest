AI-discovered PHP vulnerabilities trigger global cybersecurity emergency
Critical remote code execution vulnerabilities in PHP — the server-side language powering a huge chunk of the web — were uncovered by AI security agents and have triggered one of the most significant coordinated disclosure events in internet history. The flaws affect the entire PHP 8.x line and, in a painful twist, the coordinated disclosure process itself was leaked before patches were fully distributed, meaning exploits were already circulating in the wild before most admins knew to act. Amazon confirmed it had experienced attacks before the public warning was even issued. Governments and major enterprises are scrambling to assess their exposure; the UK government alone reportedly found 500 new vulnerabilities in its own systems in the process. OpenAI's 'Patch the Planet' initiative — a program connecting security researchers, maintainers, and incident responders — is playing a central coordination role in the response. The BBC's Zoe Kleinman aptly described PHP as 'the cyber equivalent of an unexploded bomb,' which is apt: PHP is ancient, ubiquitous, and under-resourced in terms of security maintenance. One Hacker News user broke the embargo accidentally and publicly apologized, illustrating how chaotic the disclosure process became. The PHP Foundation has published guidance on how they plan to strengthen PHP security going forward. If you're running anything on PHP, patching is not optional — exploits are active. This is a genuine crisis, not a theoretical one, and the fact that AI agents surfaced the bugs is a meaningful proof point for AI-assisted vulnerability research at scale.

eMudhra exits TLS certificate business after catastrophic key compromise
Indian certificate authority eMudhra is shutting down its global SSL/TLS certificate business after a deeply embarrassing and dangerous security failure: private keys from eight of its intermediate CA certificates were compromised during a botched key signing ceremony. Because these certificates chain up to roots trusted by Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple, and Chrome, the compromise theoretically allowed anyone holding the keys to mint valid web credentials for any site on the internet — a near-worst-case scenario for web PKI trust. The failure happened during a ceremony that is supposed to be conducted under strict physical and procedural controls, making the breach both technically severe and organizationally inexcusable. Any software that bundles a trusted certificate list — browsers, operating systems, embedded devices — needs to be updated to distrust the compromised intermediates. The incident is a stark reminder of how much of the web's security model depends on certificate authorities behaving competently, and eMudhra clearly did not. Browser vendors moved quickly to revoke trust, but the window of exposure remains a concern for anyone who may have been targeted before revocation propagated. eMudhra's exit from the TLS business is probably the right outcome — trust, once broken this badly, is nearly impossible to rebuild in the CA ecosystem. This story also highlights ongoing structural fragility in the CA/Browser Forum model: a single CA's failure can have global consequences. Security teams should audit any certificates issued by eMudhra and reissue them through a different authority as a precaution.

Quad9 has been unusually vocal about its legal battles — including a landmark case in Germany it ultimately won — but France appears to be a fight it has decided isn't worth having. For French users, this means they'll need to switch to alternative resolvers; for the broader internet, it's a warning sign about how regulation can fragment global infrastructure. The melanoma indication is significant because late-stage melanoma has historically been among the harder cancers to treat, and immunotherapy has already transformed the landscape — this vaccine appears to meaningfully improve on even the immunotherapy baseline. Personalized cancer vaccines have been in development for years, and these are the clearest efficacy data yet that the approach works at scale. For patients with high-risk melanoma, this could become a meaningful new treatment option within a few years if the full trial data and regulatory review support what the top-line numbers suggest.
