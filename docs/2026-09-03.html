OpenAI's new 'Astra' model uses a reasoning technique that's alarming AI safety researchers
OpenAI has announced a model called Astra that uses a technique called 'recurrent depth,' which lets the model reason outside the sequential, step-by-step thinking pattern that characterizes most current reasoning models. That non-sequential reasoning is what's spooking safety researchers — the concern is that a model operating in less predictable thinking loops is harder to audit, interpret, and constrain. OpenAI's own Preparedness Framework documentation confirms Astra is the first model to hit their 'Critical' cybersecurity capability threshold, which under their framework requires stronger safeguards before release. The fact that OpenAI is releasing it anyway, with what they describe as enhanced safeguards, signals confidence — but also raises the stakes if those safeguards have gaps. The timing is particularly pointed given the ongoing lawsuit that may force the Trump administration to reveal its secret AI safety testing rules (see the Policy section). Astra's recurrent depth approach is technically interesting: it potentially allows the model to iterate on its own reasoning in ways that could produce more powerful problem-solving, but at the cost of interpretability. AI safety experts have long worried about capability jumps that outpace our ability to understand what a model is actually doing internally. This is a story to watch closely — not because Astra is necessarily dangerous, but because it represents a genuine escalation in the capability-vs-safety tension that's been simmering all year.
