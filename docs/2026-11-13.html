If AI can do that work at scale, it could unlock a new era of provably correct software. The caveat, as always, is that the proofs are only as good as the specifications they're verifying against — garbage in, garbage out still applies at the spec level.

AI agents are straining zero-trust security architectures
IEEE Spectrum has a piece examining how the rise of agentic AI systems is creating new challenges for zero-trust security models. Zero-trust architecture was designed around the assumption that you can verify the identity and authorization of discrete actors — humans, services, devices — before granting access to resources. Agentic AI systems complicate this by acting autonomously, often chaining together multiple tool calls, accessing APIs, and making decisions without direct human oversight at each step. The question of how to authenticate and authorize an AI agent that is itself orchestrating other agents is genuinely hard, and existing identity frameworks weren't designed for it. The article highlights that the attack surface expands significantly when agents can dynamically acquire capabilities or make network requests as part of normal operation. This is particularly relevant as more enterprises deploy tools like OpenAI's Codex, Anthropic's Claude Code, and similar agentic coding and research assistants in production environments. Security teams are going to need new mental models and new tooling — the perimeter has gotten very fuzzy. If you're deploying agentic AI in a regulated industry, this is worth reading before your next architecture review.

New dwarf planet candidate discovered with a ~280-year orbit beyond Pluto
Astronomers have identified a new dwarf planet candidate in the outer solar system, with an orbital period of roughly 280 years — placing it well beyond Pluto in the region sometimes called the scattered disc. The Hacker News discussion has 324 upvotes, reflecting genuine public interest in solar system discoveries. Dwarf planet classification requires meeting specific size and orbital criteria set by the IAU, so 'candidate' is the right hedging word here — confirmation takes time and additional observation. The outer solar system remains one of the least well-characterized regions of our astronomical neighborhood: surveys like LSST/Rubin Observatory are systematically improving our census of these objects, and new discoveries are becoming more frequent as detection capabilities improve. Objects with orbits in this range can tell us about the early dynamical history of the solar system and the hypothesized Planet Nine. For context, Pluto has an orbital period of about 248 years, so this object is in a similar regime but distinct. It's a reminder that there are still genuine discoveries to be made in our own solar system backyard.
