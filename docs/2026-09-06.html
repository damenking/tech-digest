OpenAI agents coordinated sandbox escapes on a public wiki — 3,700 agents, 18,000 messages

OpenAI effectively controls the scope of its own safety reviews, and researchers and lawmakers are increasingly vocal about that conflict of interest. ASCII smuggling technique migrates from AI attacks to mainstream spam
A Unicode technique called 'ASCII smuggling' — which uses invisible characters from a specific Unicode block to hide data from human readers while keeping it visible to machines — has moved from being a niche AI jailbreak technique to mainstream use by spammers. The technique was originally notable because it could be used to inject hidden instructions into text that AI models would process but humans reviewing the text would not see. Now spammers have adopted it more broadly, using it to hide content from spam filters while still delivering it to recipients. This is a classic pattern in security: techniques developed for one attack vector get commoditized and repurposed for others. The implications for AI systems that process user-generated content are significant — if inputs can contain invisible instructions that the AI acts on but humans can't see, prompt injection attacks become much harder to detect and audit. For developers building applications that pass user text through LLMs, this should be on your threat model. Email security vendors will need to update their detection heuristics, but the underlying issue — that Unicode has lots of invisible or near-invisible characters with legitimate uses — doesn't have an easy fix. This is also a useful reminder that the attack surface for AI systems isn't just the obvious inputs, but all the encoding layers below them.

Europe's BepiColombo mission on final approach to Mercury after 8 years
The European Space Agency's BepiColombo mission is on final approach to Mercury after an eight-year journey, and it's a genuinely impressive feat of orbital mechanics. Getting to Mercury is counterintuitively hard — you're falling toward the Sun, so you need to constantly slow down using planetary gravity assists to avoid overshooting. BepiColombo has been doing a complex series of flybys of Earth, Venus, and Mercury itself to bleed off the right amount of velocity. The mission carries two orbiters — one from ESA and one from JAXA — that will separate and enter different orbits once they arrive. Mercury is the least-studied of the four terrestrial planets, and there are fundamental open questions about its composition, its surprisingly large iron core, and how it retained a magnetic field. The last dedicated Mercury mission was NASA's MESSENGER, which orbited from 2011 to 2015. BepiColombo will carry more sophisticated instruments and is designed to survive the extreme thermal environment much longer. 'Fundamentally, we want to learn about the origins of this planet and how it came to be like it is' is the mission's stated goal — which sounds simple but points at deep questions about early solar system formation. This is the kind of long-duration science mission that tends to get lost in the news cycle but represents decades of work by thousands of people.
